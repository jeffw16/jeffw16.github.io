If returns are different

All figures are in today's dollars using real (inflation-adjusted) returns, compounded monthly, with contributions at the end of each month. Portfolio returns are rough long-run historical averages, not guarantees. Taxes are modeled as a single effective rate on withdrawals; fees and early-withdrawal penalties are not modeled. This is an educational tool, not financial advice.

CoastFIRE

Your savings are already big enough that, with no more contributions, growth alone carries them to your full retirement number by your traditional retirement age. After that you only need to earn enough to cover your current expenses.

FIRE number

The balance that can fund retirement: annual spending (grossed up for tax, less any Social Security or pension) divided by your safe withdrawal rate. Reach it and you can retire fully.

Safe withdrawal rate

The share of your portfolio you withdraw in the first year of retirement, then adjust for inflation. 4% has historically lasted about 30 years; use a lower rate for a longer retirement.

Real return

Yearly growth after inflation. Real returns keep every figure in today's dollars. The presets are rough long-run averages, not promises.

Tax on withdrawals

A single average rate applied to 401(k) withdrawals, since traditional 401(k) money is taxed as income. Spending is grossed up so you net the amount you entered. Use 0% for Roth money.

Social Security bridge

If you retire before Social Security or a pension starts, your 401(k) has to cover your full spending until it does. The calculator adds enough for those bridge years, then counts the benefit against spending afterward.

Contribute until coast, then stop

The dotted line shows your balance if you keep contributing until your CoastFIRE age and then stop completely. It should reach your FIRE number by your traditional retirement age.

Simulated chances

These figures come from 1,000 simulated markets that share your expected return but bounce around from year to year, or from replaying every starting year of US stock and bond history since 1928. A chance is the share of those markets where the plan worked; 90% confidence is a safer bar than the single steady-returns line.
